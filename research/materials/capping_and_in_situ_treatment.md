# Capping and in-situ treatment of contaminated sediment

Retrieved 2026-09-08.

## US EPA, definition of a reactive cap

* Document: *Climate Change Adaptation Technical Fact Sheet: Contaminated Sediment
  Remedies*, EPA 542-F-15-009, Office of Superfund Remediation and Technology
  Innovation, April 2015.
* URL: <https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2018-08/documents/contaminated_sediments.pdf>
* Retrieval: `FETCHED_PDF`, pages 1 and 2 read directly.

Verbatim, page 1:

> "Capping (in situ capping) involves placing clean material on top of the contaminated
> material. A cap often consists of several layers of various materials, such as an
> isolation layer of sand and/or soil and an armor layer of gravel, cobbles, and/or large
> boulders. In some cases it includes a habitat layer designed to mimic the native
> sediment and promote recovery of benthic communities. In a reactive cap, the isolation
> layer includes an amendment (such as organoclay or activated carbon mats) that binds or
> sequesters contaminants exiting the sediment pore water, thereby preventing contaminant
> release to surface water."

Verbatim, page 2, on physical failure of a cap:

> "Potential scour of a sediment cap or underlying sediment due to an increase in surface
> water flow velocity and/or turbulence associated with intense storms or sustained
> freeze conditions"

Why it matters here: the EPA definition already names the mat form, so no novelty is
available for it. The scour sentence is independent support for degradation mode 3 in
`docs/MODEL_SPEC.md` section 4.

## ITRC sediment cap guidance

* ITRC *Sediment Cap* guidance, chapter 7, monitoring and maintenance objectives and
  approaches. URL: <https://sd-1.itrcweb.org/7-monitoring-and-maintenance-objectives-and-approaches/>
* Also: ITRC *Contaminated Sediments Remedy Selection*, chapter 5, conventional and
  amended capping. URL: <https://projects.itrcweb.org/contseds_remedy-selection/Content/5%20Conventional%20and%20Amended%20Capping.htm>
* Retrieval: `FETCH_403` for both, on 2026-09-08.

Search-index summary only, therefore **not verbatim**: long-term contaminant migration
through caps occurs by advection, diffusion or dispersion of porewater, by bioturbation,
or by ebullition; porewater sampling is the preferred way to monitor cap performance;
long-term monitoring includes bathymetry, periodic cap and sediment sampling, porewater
sampling and surface-water sampling; inspections should be frequent in the first six
months after cap placement because settling problems appear then.

Recorded as a lead to obtain properly. Nothing in this repository should cite ITRC
wording until the document is actually retrieved.

## EU policy position

* LIFE SEDREMED (LIFE20 ENV/IT/000572), *Technological and policy solutions for the
  management of contaminated sediments in the EU*, event dated 9 February 2023.
* URL: <https://life-sedremed.eu/technological-and-policy-solutions-for-the-management-of-contaminated-sediments-in-the-eu/>
* Retrieval: `FETCHED_OK`.

Verbatim: "the current legal frameworks are fragmented across the EU, and Member States
address the challenges with diverging approaches". The page also records expert agreement
that dredging and capping "is expensive and cannot be applied everywhere", and that no
EU-wide sediment-specific environmental quality standards exist.

Why it matters here: it is the honest reason a cheaper, smaller-footprint, serviceable
option is worth studying at all, and it is also the reason no European regulatory
acceptance can be assumed.

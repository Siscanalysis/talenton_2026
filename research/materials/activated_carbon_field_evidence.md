Retrieved 2026-09-08.

## Scale of deployment

* Patmont, C.R., Ghosh, U., LaRosa, P., Menzie, C.A., Luthy, R.G., Greenberg, M.S.,
  Cornelissen, G., Eek, E., Collins, J., Hull, J., Hjartland, T., Glaza, E., Bleiler, J.
  and Quadrini, J. (2015), *In situ sediment treatment using activated carbon: a
  demonstrated sediment cleanup technology*, Integrated Environmental Assessment and
  Management 11(2):195-207.
* URL used: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4409844/> (`FETCHED_OK`).
  The Wiley full-text page returned HTTP 403 on the same date.

Verbatim: "Through 2013, however, more than 25 field-scale demonstrations or full-scale
projects spanning a range of environmental conditions were completed or underway in the
United States, Norway, and the Netherlands."

Delivery methods described: direct application by slurry injection, pneumatic spreading
and mechanical mixing; blended cover with clean sand or sediment; and engineered
composites, specifically SediMite (about 50 per cent activated carbon with binding agents)
and AquaGate (dense aggregate with clay-sized materials and polymeric additives).

* Earlier field application: Cho, Y.-M., Ghosh, U., Kennedy, A.J., Grossman, A., Ray, G.,
  Tomaszewski, J.E., Smithenry, D.W., Bridges, T.S. and Luthy, R.G. (2009), *Field
  application of activated carbon amendment for in-situ stabilization of polychlorinated
  biphenyls in marine sediment*, Environmental Science and Technology, published
  15 May 2009. Hunters Point, San Francisco Bay. Retrieval: `SEARCH_INDEX_ONLY` (ACS page
  and PubMed entry indexed; full text not retrieved).

## Grenland fjords, Norway: the largest European trial, and its warnings

### Deployment and physical fate

* Samuelsson, G.S., Raymond, C., Agrenius, S., Schaanning, M., Cornelissen, G. and
  Gunnarsson, J.S. (2017), *Response of marine benthic fauna to thin-layer capping with
  activated carbon in a large-scale field experiment in the Grenland fjords, Norway*,
  Environmental Science and Pollution Research 24(16):14218-14233,
  DOI 10.1007/s11356-017-8851-6.
* URL: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5486621/> (`FETCHED_OK`).

Verbatim fragments:

> "three test fields (100 x 100 m)" at 30 m depth; "one test field (200 x 200 m)" at 95 m
> depth

> "The achieved thickness of the caps were after 1 month measured to 11 +/- 6 and
> 12 +/- 3 mm for AC+clay-30 and AC+clay-95 respectively."

> activated carbon mixed at a "1:10 dw/dw ratio with clay"

> "After 9 months, the final AC concentration was 2% by dry weight of sediment measured in
> the AC-treated fields."

> "AC+clay reduced the abundance, biomass, and number of species by up to 90% at both 30
> and 95 m depth"

with "few indications of recovery" during the study period.

Note the achieved thickness: 11 to 12 mm, with a standard deviation of 6 mm at the shallow
site. A designed 25 mm cap came out at about half the design thickness with more than
50 per cent relative variability at one site. Any assumption in our repository that a mat
lies at a uniform designed thickness is optimistic relative to this field result.

### Loss of amendment during placement

* Raymond, C., Samuelsson, G.S., Agrenius, S., Schaanning, M.T. and Gunnarsson, J.S.
  (2020), *Impaired benthic macrofauna function 4 years after sediment capping with
  activated carbon in the Grenland fjords, Norway*, Environmental Science and Pollution
  Research 28(13):16181-16197, DOI 10.1007/s11356-020-11607-0.
* URL: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969561/> (`FETCHED_OK`).
  The Springer page redirected to an authorisation endpoint and was not used.

Verbatim: during application "AC particles did not settle as expected and up to 75% were
lost to surrounding areas due to lateral advection". Residual activated carbon was still
measurable down to 5 cm depth at 49 months, so retention within the treated area was
partial rather than nil.

This is degradation mode 3 (displacement) observed at field scale. It also shows that the
loss can occur **at placement**, not only over the service life. Our `MatTileState` starts
every tile intact at `t = 0`, which is an optimistic initial condition, and that should be
stated wherever coverage is reported.

### Ecological effect, four years later

Verbatim from the 2020 abstract:

> "The capping with AC strongly reduced the benthic species diversity, abundance, and
> biomass by up to 90%."

> "Vital functions in the benthic ecosystem such as particle reworking and bioirrigation
> of the sediment were also reduced."

> "Much of the initial effects observed after 1 and 14 months were still present after 49
> months, indicating that the effects are long-lasting."

> "These long-lasting negative ecological effects should be carefully considered before
> decisions are made on sediment remediation with powdered AC, especially in large areas,
> since important ecosystem functions can be impaired."

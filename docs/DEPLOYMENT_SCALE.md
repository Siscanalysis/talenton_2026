# How much seabed is there, and what would covering it take

Every number here is generated by `src/reactive_seabed_mat/deployment/scale.py`
from published, officially designated areas. Nothing in this document proposes a
deployment, asserts that any named site is a target for this technology, or
simulates, locates or recommends handling munitions. Real work near historical
marine munitions requires specialist and environmental approval.

The point of the document is a comparison the physics cannot make. The reactive
layer answers *does a square metre of mat work*. This answers *how many square
metres are there*, and the two answers point in opposite directions.

---

## 1. The documented scale

About **40,000 tonnes of chemical munitions**, containing roughly **15,000
tonnes of chemical warfare agents**, were dumped in the Baltic after the Second
World War, and the total including conventional ammunition is put at more than
**100,000 tonnes** [B1]. The deep basins chosen were east of Bornholm,
south-east of Gotland, and the Skagerrak, all in water deeper than 70 m [B1].
EMODnet Human Activities publishes the dumping areas on its map of dumped
munitions [B2].

The **Bornholm Basin primary dumpsite** is the best-defined of them: a circle of
radius **3 nautical miles**, with a rectangular secondary area around it whose
extent is close to **1000 km²**. About 32,000 tonnes of chemical weapons
containing about 11,000 tonnes of agents went into it in 1947 [B3].

A 3-nautical-mile circle is **96.98 km²**.

## 2. What covering it would take

At this demonstrator's assumed mat, 4.0 kg/m² of keratin core and an assumed
EUR 240/m² of mat material:

| Area | km² | Sorbent | Mat material | Share of one year's world wool clip | Vessel days | Times this demo's hotspot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| This demonstrator's hypothetical hotspot | 0.0064 | 26 t | EUR 1.5 M | 0.001 % | 0.3 | 1 |
| **Bornholm primary dumpsite** | **96.98** | **388,000 t** | **EUR 23.3 bn** | **22 %** | **4,849** | **15,153** |
| Bornholm extended area | 1000 | 4.0 Mt | EUR 240 bn | 227 % | 50,000 | 156,250 |
| Gotland Deep area | 1500 | 6.0 Mt | EUR 360 bn | 341 % | 75,000 | 234,375 |
| Skagerrak areas | 1300 | 5.2 Mt | EUR 312 bn | 296 % | 65,000 | 203,125 |

The vessel-day column assumes **two hectares laid per day**, which is a
deliberately optimistic rate for precision placement at 90 m depth and is chosen
so the figure understates rather than overstates the difficulty. No contractor
has been asked. Every euro value is an assumption from `CostConfig`; no supplier
has been contacted and no quotation exists.

World greasy wool production is about **1.76 Mt** (FAO, 2022) [B4]. The older,
smaller figure is used so the "share of the world clip" column is the larger,
less flattering one.

## 3. What the table means

**Area capping of a munitions dumpsite is not a plan.** Covering the Bornholm
primary dumpsite alone would consume roughly a fifth of a year's global wool
clip and cost tens of billions of euro in material before a single vessel sailed.
It would take more than thirteen years of continuous laying at a laying rate
nobody has demonstrated. The extended areas are worse by another order of
magnitude, and the Skagerrak sites sit in 400 m of water, far deeper than any
capping technology has been deployed in.

Anyone presenting this technology as a way to clean up the Baltic is not doing
arithmetic.

**What the numbers do support** is targeted covering of small, identified,
high-flux patches. For orientation, at the assumed material price:

| Material budget | Area | |
|---|---|---|
| EUR 1 M | 4,167 m² | 0.42 ha |
| EUR 5 M | 20,833 m² | 2.08 ha |
| EUR 20 M | 83,333 m² | 8.33 ha |
| EUR 100 M | 416,667 m² | 41.7 ha |

A realistic first deployment is **one to two hectares**. That is three to five
times this demonstrator's hypothetical hotspot, and about 0.02 % of the Bornholm
primary dumpsite.

**Which reframes what the project is actually for.** If only a few hectares can
ever be covered, then the binding question is not "how well does the mat work"
but "**which few hectares**". That is a targeting and monitoring problem, and it
is the same problem the evidence loop in this repository already addresses: you
cannot maintain what you cannot see, and you certainly cannot choose where to
spend two hectares of mat without knowing where the flux actually is.

The honest positioning is therefore:

* not a remediation technology for a dumpsite;
* possibly a containment technology for a hotspot, if one can be located and the
  material survives;
* definitely a monitoring and decision problem first.

## 4. What this section does not do

It does not estimate how much of the contamination sits in the small fraction of
area that could be covered. That fraction matters enormously: if 80 % of the
flux comes from 1 % of the area, targeted covering is worth doing, and if the
flux is spread evenly it is not. **No dataset was found that resolves benthic
metal flux spatially at a dumpsite**, which is the same gap recorded as `GAP1`
in `research/references/datasets.json`. Until that is measured, the case for
targeted deployment rests on an assumption about spatial heterogeneity that
nobody in this project has tested.

It also does not cost the vessel, the survey, the permitting, the monitoring
programme or the eventual retrieval of loaded media. The material cost is the
smallest of those, and it is already prohibitive at dumpsite scale.

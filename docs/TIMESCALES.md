# Time scales: what the answer depends on

Generated by `tools/timescale_check.py` in 1892 s,
on scenario `progressive_saturation`. Every number below is computed, not typed.

Four different questions travel under the heading "check the time scales", and
this project has already been burned by conflating two of them. The first
reactive-layer scheme conserved mass to one part in 1e14 and was still wrong:
refining the step moved the predicted breakthrough from 4.2 years to 1.0 years.
**Mass conservation is not convergence.**

**Spread across the whole refinement: 2.39e-05 in attenuation.** The default
step of 6 h is inside that spread, so it is converged for this purpose. The mass
imbalance column is a separate check and passing it would prove nothing on its
own.

Quoting a one-year attenuation as if it were a service life is the single
easiest way to mislead with this model.

## 3. Plume window: is the short 2-D run long enough?

The mat state is held fixed and the coastal field is integrated for a few hours.
If the window were too short the answer would still be moving.

| window (h) | peak with mat (ng/L) | peak without (ng/L) | ratio | mass imbalance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 110.6 | 175.8 | 0.6291 | 0.00e+00 |
| 6 | 27.86 | 37.63 | 0.7404 | 0.00e+00 |
| 12 | 41.85 | 57.1 | 0.7330 | 0.00e+00 |
| 24 | 43.66 | 59.99 | 0.7278 | 0.00e+00 |
| 48 | 67.1 | 101.7 | 0.6595 | 0.00e+00 |

(M2 cycles spanned: 0.24, 0.48, 0.97, 1.93, 3.87.)

**This one does not converge, and that is the finding.** The forcing is tidal
with an M2 period of 12.42 h, so the field never reaches a steady state: it
oscillates. A *peak* concentration sampled at the end of a window therefore
depends on which tidal phase the window happens to end in, and none of the
windows above spans a whole number of cycles. The peak swings by a factor of
four across the table and the with-mat to without-mat ratio spans about 0.11.

Three consequences, and they are not all bad:

* The **mass ledgers are unaffected**. The imbalance column is zero at every
  window length, because conservation does not care about tidal phase.
* The **map snapshots are unaffected**. They are states at an instant, not
  averages, and each is labelled with its time.
* The **peak-ratio metric is window-dependent**. It should not be quoted to more
  than one significant figure, and never compared between runs with different
  window lengths. A cycle-averaged or cycle-integrated metric would be the right
  fix. It is not implemented, and this is recorded as a limitation rather than
  papered over.

## 4. Decision cadence: does the maintenance outcome depend on how often you look?

Four simulated years. The chemistry arrives on a 90-day sampling interval with a
21-day laboratory latency, so a decision period well below 90 days cannot add
information, only decisions.

| decision period (d) | decisions | services | observations | Pb attenuation | assumed cost (EUR) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 97 | 0 | 25,035 | 0.9452 | 0 |
| 30 | 48 | 0 | 21,753 | 0.9452 | 0 |
| 90 | 16 | 0 | 19,741 | 0.9452 | 0 |
| 180 | 8 | 0 | 19,315 | 0.9452 | 0 |
| 365 | 4 | 0 | 19,770 | 0.9452 | 0 |

Twenty-four times as many decisions between the fastest and slowest cadence, and
**the outcome is identical**: same attenuation to four figures, zero services in
every case, and barely more observations. Deciding more often than the evidence
arrives produces more decisions and not more information.

That is worth showing, because "monitor continuously" is an easy thing to
promise and a poor use of a monitoring budget when the binding constraint is a
quarterly grab sample and a three-week laboratory queue. The zero in the
services column is the same finding as in `docs/AUDIT.md` section E, seen from
another angle: this monitoring programme cannot support a replacement decision
at *any* cadence, so making decisions more often does not help. Instrumenting
more tiles or measuring the seepage would; looking more often at the same thin
evidence would not.
